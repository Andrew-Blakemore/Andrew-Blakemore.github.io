    <h2>Prosthetic Care Across a Divided System</h2>

    <p class="itinerary-story__dek">
      My time in the United Kingdom was less a single journey through one prosthetics
      network than a collection of experiences spread across the country. I spent most
      of my time in and around London and Leeds, combining conversations with clinicians
      with a visit to a prosthetics clinic and some less expected encounters with
      prosthetics along the way. I had also hoped to travel into Scotland, but the
      logistics ultimately did not work out. Instead, some of those conversations
      continued remotely, allowing me to hear perspectives from beyond the places I
      could physically reach.
    </p>

    <p class="itinerary-story__dek">
      One theme connected nearly all of these experiences: the relationship between
      Britain's National Health Service and private prosthetic care. The NHS provides
      prosthetic services through a network of specialist rehabilitation and limb
      centres, making care available without the insurance structure that shapes so
      much of the American system. But private prosthetic care exists alongside it,
      offering another route to treatment and, in some cases, access to different
      components, services, or timelines. The distinction is not simply a matter of
      one system replacing the other. Understanding British prosthetic care means
      looking at how these pathways coexist, what each can provide, and how clinicians
      and prosthetic users navigate the differences between them.
    </p>
