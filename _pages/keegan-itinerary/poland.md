      <h2 id="justspeak">Twelve People, Plenty to Talk About</h2>
      <p>When I met the rest of the group, the first thing that struck me was how different we all were. There were twelve of us, six English speakers and six Polish students. The other tutors came from Canada, Ireland, France, and Turkey, while the students brought a wide range of interests, backgrounds, and levels of English. Some of us had recently graduated from university. Others had been retired for years.</p>
      <figure class="itinerary-story__body-copy">
        <img src="{{ '/assets/img/keegan/poland/justspeak-group.webp' | relative_url }}" alt="Our English immersion group gathered around a pumpkin display." width="1536" height="1152" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="height: auto; max-height: none;">
        <figcaption>The whole crew, with a few seasonal additions.</figcaption>

      <br>
      <p>The program was straightforward enough. Each day, we paired up for one-on-one conversations about topics the students chose. Between those sessions, we came together for matching games, charades, Apples to Apples, and other group activities. There was no shortage of opportunities to talk.</p>
      <figure class="itinerary-story__body-copy">
        <img src="{{ '/assets/img/keegan/poland/word-games.webp' | relative_url }}" alt="Five participants sitting around a table with letter tiles during a group activity." width="1536" height="1152" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="height: auto; max-height: none;">
        <figcaption>Putting our vocabulary to work.</figcaption>
      </figure>
      <br>
      <p>After several weeks of staying in hostels in Iceland and the UK, followed by a friend’s apartment in Berlin, this was a change of pace. Here, getting to know the people around me was practically the entire schedule.</p>

      <h2 id="people">The People Behind the Practice</h2>
      <p>My favorite part, by far, was getting to know the Polish students. Our conversations gave me time to hear about their lives and understand what had brought them to an English camp in the first place.</p>
      <p>One had been a teacher before becoming a tour guide at Auschwitz after retirement. She had led tours in French and German for years and was now working toward certification to give them in English, too. During our sessions, she treated me as a member of her tour group, walking me through information she could already comfortably explain in three other languages. I had come to help her practice English and found myself listening to a history lesson. Hearing how she approached that responsibility as a Polish guide gave me a perspective I would not have gotten from reading about it on my own.</p>
      <figure class="itinerary-story__body-copy" style="max-width: 465px;">
        <img src="{{ '/assets/img/keegan/poland/english-conversation.webp' | relative_url }}" alt="An outdoor one-on-one conversation during the English immersion program." width="1152" height="1536" loading="lazy" decoding="async" style="height: auto; max-height: none;">
        <figcaption>One of our afternoon conversations.</figcaption>
      </figure>
      <br>
      <p>Another student was an interventional cardiologist who performed procedures such as placing stents. He wanted to improve his English so he could present at international conferences, though I initially wondered how much help he needed. He spoke naturally and confidently, with a medical vocabulary that was especially impressive.</p>
      <p>Then I learned that he was entirely self-taught. Over the previous few years, he had practiced a little each day, watching shows and listening to podcasts related to his field. The ease with which he spoke made it hard to believe he had learned the language so recently.</p>
      <p>As a former pre-med student, I especially looked forward to our conversations. We talked about the heart, the procedures he performed, and his path through medical training in Poland. I had enough background to follow along and plenty of questions to keep us occupied.</p>
      <p>I’m glad I took a week to do this. Twelve people who might otherwise never have met had plenty to say to one another, once we had a reason to sit down and talk. I was there to help with English, but the conversations gave me just as much to think about.</p>
    </section>
